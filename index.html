For Sons, Daughters & Spouses

When you love an aging parent, the hardest part is what happens between your visits.

You’re juggling work, your own household, and constant concern for a parent in their 80s or 90s. Even when you visit as often as you can, it’s hard not to wonder:

? “Is Mom lonely spending long afternoons sitting by herself?”
? “Is Dad actually cooking and eating proper, nutritious meals?”
? “Will my parent accept help—or feel like we’re taking away their independence?”
? “How can I catch my breath or travel for winter without feeling guilty?”
The Warm First Step

Not every family needs clinical nursing. Often, what changes everything is a trusted friend who looks forward to visiting.

Calling a clinical home-care agency can make a proud parent feel like they are “losing independence.” Welcoming a warm companion from Her Helping Hand to bake together, tend the garden, head out for coffee in St. Jacobs, or run groceries feels like adding to their life.

The “I Don’t Need Help!” Parent

How we help proud older adults welcome a companion—without feeling “managed.”

Many 85- and 90-year-olds tell their children: “I don’t want a stranger in my house looking after me.” That’s why we never show up acting like clinical supervisors. We start around an activity your parent genuinely enjoys—gardening, baking, a drive for coffee, or help organizing the house.

  • Start small with a low-pressure half-day or single weekly visit
  • Framed around shared interests, household partnership, or a ride to errands
  • Families frequently expand from 1 visit/week to 2–3 visits/week once their parent falls in love with their companion

Dementia-Friendly Engagement

Gentle familiarity, music, patience, and calm routines.

For a parent living with memory loss or dementia, rotating agency staff creates confusion and anxiety. Our approach centers on emotional warmth, familiar faces, favourite music, unhurried walks, and meeting your loved one right where they are each day.

  • Music videos, favourite songs, coffee chats, cards, and gentle walks
  • Attentive observation of appetite, mood, and daily wellbeing shared with family
  • Flexible accompaniment to short-notice doctor or lab appointments

Meet Our Leader

Personal, owner-led care—never a revolving door of strangers.

Andy graduated from Western University (UWO) and began her career in corporate sales at Coca-Cola, where she loved the lasting relationships she formed with clients. After leaving the corporate world to raise her three children, Andy launched Her Helping Hand to bring warm, relationship-first companionship to older adults across Waterloo Region.

Andy is a caregiver by nature. As a young adult, she loved visiting her grandma out of town to hear stories of her childhood in the early 1900s. When her Nanny passed away, Andy moved in with her Pops to help around the house, prepare meals, play gin rummy, and keep him company. A longtime volunteer senior fitness instructor with Meals on Wheels, Andy lives in Waterloo with her husband Geoff, their three children, dog, and two cats.

Our 4 Codified Service Standards

1. Personal Fit First

We learn about your loved one’s personality, life history, and interests before visits ever begin.

2. Companion Continuity

We prioritize matching your parent with a consistent, familiar companion they genuinely look forward to seeing.

3. Family Communication

With your parent’s consent, we agree in advance on how and when you receive post-visit updates and observations.

4. Reliable Scheduling

Clear visit confirmations and warm, trusted team continuity if schedules ever need to adjust.
